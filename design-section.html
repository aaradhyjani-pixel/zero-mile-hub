Three cities, one roof

VSI is not a new airport with three themed zones. It is one building whose parts each come from one of the three airports that closed to make it. Its structure is Mumbai's: branching columns that flare into a ceiling of coffers, each coffer a skylight with a coloured lens, and a 40 m cantilever over the kerb, as at Mumbai's Terminal 2. Its underside is Bengaluru's: bamboo-clad soffits, hanging gardens, a planted valley where the Hall becomes the Mall, and a garden court at every pier root, after Bengaluru's terminal in a garden. Its edges are Ahmedabad's: jaali screens shading every sun-facing glass wall, and a forecourt that steps down like a stepwell to shaded water.

Each gateway points home

The Zero Mile Hall keeps its seven lobes, like the segments of a Nagpur orange, but turns so one lobe flows due east into the Mall. The three lobes facing the city then point within 8° of the airports they replace: south to Bengaluru, west-south-west to Mumbai, west-north-west to Ahmedabad. Each is that city's check-in hall, in its materials and art. Their axes meet under the oculus at the Zero Mile marker, where a floor inlay recalls Sardar Patel bringing more than 560 princely states into one India. The two western lobes also frame the year's sunsets, 245° in December to 295° in June, so the hardest sun lands on the jaali, not the glass.

Runways

Six east-west runways in two triples either side of a 3.6 km midfield. Middle to inner runways are 1,035 m apart, the ICAO minimum for independent approaches with modern surveillance, so the runway desk can turn any runway to arrivals. Outer to middle is 1,550 m. By default three land and three take off. The 4,500 m inner runway handles heavy jets on 47 °C afternoons: ICAO's heat and height corrections take a 777-300ER's 3.1 km sea-level take-off to about 4.3 km here.

Taxiing in loops

End-around taxiways at both ends mean no aircraft crosses a runway. Taxiways are one-way loops: the outer parallels run west, and the midfield has two parallels, west by the runway and east by the terminal, so nobody meets head-on. A widebody exit set sits beyond the narrowbody rapid exits. Holding bays take aircraft whose stand isn't free yet, and a heat pad by the long runway lets heavy jets step aside.

Heat first

Every skylight is a north-facing coffer: daylight without direct summer sun. They cover about 5.5% of the roof, against about 12% at Bengaluru's T2, because Nagpur reaches 47.9 °C. Deep overhangs and jaali on the east and west faces, a heavy stone floor, cool air delivered low and drawn out high, and the roof's valleys collect about 1.45 million m³ of monsoon rain a year (estimates).

Transfers and phasing

Airlines live in neighbourhoods, so most domestic connections stay within one concourse pair: about 7 to 11 minutes door to door. International to domestic runs through a transfer centre with immigration and re-screening: about 25 minutes. The people mover runs every 90 seconds under the Mall. Bengaluru moves in first, then Ahmedabad with Hyderabad's banks, then Mumbai, then Delhi's banks: the game's four levels. Phase 2 adds two satellites on the east apron for 260 million passengers.

Why Vikram Sarabhai

Vikram Sarabhai (1919–1971) is called the father of India's space programme. An Ahmedabad man, he founded the Physical Research Laboratory and helped found IIM Ahmedabad, and he picked Thumba, on the magnetic equator near Thiruvananthapuram, for India's first rocket range. On 21 November 1963 a two-stage Nike-Apache sounding rocket supplied by NASA lifted off from there. The team turned a church dedicated to St Mary Magdalene into its workshop and the bishop's house into its office, and carried rocket parts and payloads to the pad by bicycle and bullock cart. This airport is named for that spirit: impossible infrastructure, started with almost nothing.

A bicycle and a rocket in the Hall

Just east of the oculus, on the walk from the Zero Mile marker into the Mall, a bicycle stands on the stone floor with a rocket section strapped to its carrier, full size and unlabelled except for one line cut into the plinth: A bicycle carrying a rocket section, after the photographs of parts brought to Thumba by bicycle in the 1960s. Every passenger who crosses the Hall walks past it.
